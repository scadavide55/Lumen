moved in one step.

Tacet reads your Spaces, Favorites, pinned tabs and folders straight from Arc and rebuilds them as tab groups and pinned tabs, with your history, bookmarks and sign-ins. Vertical tabs, a command bar, split view and an assistant that can run on your Mac are already there.

Download Tacet Compare with Arc

Three steps, about a minute

Download Tacet

Open the .dmg and drag Tacet to Applications. It's free and open source, for macOS 14 or later.

Choose Arc

The setup lists the browsers on your Mac: pick Arc and keep “Arc Spaces and pinned tabs” ticked. Later, use File › Import from Another Browser.

Allow, then browse

When macOS asks about “Arc Safe Storage”, choose Allow to keep your sign-ins. Your Spaces appear as folded tab groups that load only when you open them.

Questions from Arc users

Do I need to do this before December 22?

Yes. Tacet reads Arc's files on your Mac, and Arc may remove them once it stops working. Importing now is safest; you can keep using Arc until then.

Will it open hundreds of tabs at once?

No. Imported tabs wait folded in their groups and load only when you click them, so a big sidebar doesn't slow your Mac down.

Is Tacet built on Chromium, like Arc?

No, on WebKit, the engine of Safari. Sites behave as they do in Safari, with its speed and battery life. Chrome extensions are supported on macOS 15.4 or later, as are Safari and Firefox ones.

What does it cost? Who's behind it?

Nothing. Tacet is free and open source under the GPL-3.0, has no accounts and no analytics, and is made by one person. The code is on GitHub.

macOS says Tacet is damaged. What now?

Tacet isn't notarized by Apple yet. The download is fine: run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tacet.app once in Terminal, then open it normally.
